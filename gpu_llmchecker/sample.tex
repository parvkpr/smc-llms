% ── Abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Probabilistic Computation Tree Logic (PCTL) model checking of Large Language
Model (LLM) text generation provides formal, probability-certified answers to
questions such as \emph{``What is the probability this model generates
gender-biased text in the next $L$ tokens?''}
The recently proposed \textsc{LLMChecker} framework~\cite{gross2025llmchecker}
demonstrates this is feasible for small lookahead depths, but is bottlenecked by
state-space explosion: the $\alpha$-$k$-bounded DTMC has up to $k^L$ states,
and exact verification via the Storm model checker becomes intractable beyond
$L \approx 10$ tokens.

We make three contributions to break this barrier.
\textbf{(1)} We replace the recursive depth-first DTMC construction
(Algorithm~1 in~\cite{gross2025llmchecker}) with a \emph{breadth-first}
construction that submits all nodes at depth $d$ as a single batched GPU
inference call, reducing serial LLM calls from $\bigO(k^L)$ to $\bigO(L)$.
\textbf{(2)} We exploit the tree structure of the resulting DTMC to replace
Storm's iterative linear system solver with a single \emph{GPU sparse backward
induction} pass ($\bigO(L)$ sparse matrix–vector products), reducing
verification from hours to milliseconds.
\textbf{(3)} We introduce \emph{Direct Statistical Model Checking} (Direct
SMC), a novel method that bypasses DTMC construction entirely by sampling $M$
complete trajectories of length $L$ directly from the LLM using vLLM's native
multi-sequence GPU batching, then evaluating the PCTL property on each
trajectory.
With a Chernoff–Hoeffding confidence bound of
$\eps = \sqrt{\ln(2/\delta)/(2M)}$, Direct SMC scales to \emph{any} $L$
bounded only by the model's context window, requires no modification to the
LLM, and is embarrassingly parallel on GPU.
We demonstrate the system on GPT-2, obtaining formal probability estimates for
PCTL properties at $L = 50$ tokens in under $16$~seconds — a regime entirely
out of reach for the exact approach.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large Language Models generate text by sampling tokens sequentially from a
learned conditional distribution.
This sequential, probabilistic process has a natural mathematical
representation: a \emph{Discrete-Time Markov Chain} (DTMC) whose states are
the partially generated strings and whose transition probabilities are the
token probabilities output by the model.
\emph{Probabilistic Computation Tree Logic}
(PCTL)~\cite{hansson1994logic,baier2008principles} is a temporal logic
designed precisely for reasoning about properties of DTMCs, and offers a
vocabulary rich enough to express diverse safety and quality properties of LLM
outputs:

\begin{align*}
\Prob(\mathbf{F}\; \textit{gender} > 0)
  &\quad \text{``probability of eventually generating male-biased text''} \\
\Prob(\mathbf{G}\; \textit{readability} > 5997)
  &\quad \text{``probability of always producing readable text''} \\
\Prob(\mathbf{F}\; \textit{step}=5 \wedge \textit{similarity} > 90)
  &\quad \text{``probability of reproducing a copyrighted phrase''}
\end{align*}

These are not empirical statistics collected over a test set — they are formal,
probability-certified statements about the model's generation mechanism,
analogous to hardware circuit verification or safety proofs in reactive systems.

\paragraph{The \textsc{LLMChecker} paper.}
Gross, Spieker and Gotlieb~\cite{gross2025llmchecker} introduced
\textsc{LLMChecker}, the first system to apply PCTL model checking to LLM text
generation.
Their central insight is the $\alpha$-$k$ bounding trick: at each generation
step, instead of expanding all $|\Sigma| \approx 32{,}000$ possible tokens
(which makes the DTMC intractable), they retain only the top-$k$ tokens whose
cumulative probability mass reaches threshold $\alpha$.
Empirically, LLMs concentrate most of their probability on very few tokens
(e.g.\ Gemma-2B reaches $\alpha=0.9$ with the top $3$ tokens), so $k$ can be
kept small.
The resulting DTMC is encoded in the PRISM language and verified by the Storm
model checker~\cite{hensel2022storm}.

\paragraph{The state-space explosion problem.}
Despite the $\alpha$-$k$ reduction, the DTMC still has up to
$\sum_{d=0}^{L} k^d = \Theta(k^L)$ states.
For Mistral-7B with $k{=}15$, $L{=}5$: already $255{,}085$ states and
$23{,}023$ seconds of encoding time (Table~II in~\cite{gross2025llmchecker}).
For $L{=}30$, $k{=}5$: $5^{30} \approx 9.3 \times 10^{20}$ states — beyond
any computer.
The paper's authors acknowledge scalability as a key open problem.

\paragraph{Our contributions.}
We identify that two properties of the \textsc{LLMChecker} DTMC have gone
unexploited:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Level independence.}  All nodes at depth $d$ are mutually
    independent — they share no state and require identical operations (one
    LLM forward pass each).  This is natural parallelism that the DFS
    algorithm wastes.
  \item \textbf{Tree structure.}  Because each DTMC state is a unique string
    prefix, there are no back-edges or cycles.  PCTL reachability on a tree
    reduces to simple backward induction, not iterative linear system solving.
\end{enumerate}
Exploiting (1) gives our BFS construction (\S\ref{sec:bfs}).
Exploiting (2) gives our GPU sparse backward induction (\S\ref{sec:backward}).
When $k^L$ itself is the barrier, we introduce Direct SMC (\S\ref{sec:dsmc})
which avoids building the DTMC at all.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Discrete-Time Markov Chains}

\begin{definition}[DTMC]
A \emph{discrete-time Markov chain} is a tuple
$(S, P, s_0, \mathit{AP}, L)$ where $S$ is a finite set of states,
$P: S \times S \to [0,1]$ is a transition function satisfying
$\sum_{s' \in S} P(s,s') = 1$ for all $s$,
$s_0 \in S$ is the initial state,
$\mathit{AP}$ is a set of atomic propositions, and
$L: S \to 2^{\mathit{AP}}$ is a labelling function.
\end{definition}

A state $s$ is \emph{terminal} if $P(s,s)=1$ (absorbing self-loop).
A \emph{path} in the DTMC is a sequence $s_0, s_1, s_2, \ldots$ where
$P(s_i, s_{i+1}) > 0$ for all $i$.

\subsection{PCTL}

PCTL extends CTL with a probabilistic operator $\mathcal{P}_{\sim p}(\cdot)$
quantifying path properties~\cite{hansson1994logic}.
We focus on the two path formulas used in~\cite{gross2025llmchecker}:

\begin{align}
  \mathcal{P}_{\sim p}(\mathbf{F}\,\phi)
    &\quad \text{the probability of \emph{eventually} reaching a state
                 satisfying $\phi$ is $\sim p$} \label{eq:eventually}\\
  \mathcal{P}_{\sim p}(\mathbf{G}\,\phi)
    &\quad \text{the probability of \emph{always} staying in states
                 satisfying $\phi$ is $\sim p$} \label{eq:always}
\end{align}

where $\sim\, \in \{>, \ge, <, \le, =\}$ and $\phi$ is an atomic proposition
over state features (e.g.\ $\textit{gender} > 0$).

In our setting the quantified probability is \emph{computed} (not just checked
against a threshold), so we drop $\sim p$ and write $\Prob(\mathbf{F}\,\phi)$
and $\Prob(\mathbf{G}\,\phi)$ for the exact reachability probability.

\subsection{LLM Formalisation and $\alpha$-$k$ Bounding}

Following~\cite{gross2025llmchecker}, an LLM is a function
$f: \Sigma^* \to \Distr(\Sigma)$ mapping a string to a probability distribution
over tokens, where $\Sigma$ is the token vocabulary.
The $\alpha$-$k$ selection operator retains the most probable tokens up to
cumulative probability $\alpha$, capped at $k$:
\[
  \topka(f(\omega))
  = \{(\sigma_i, p_i)\}_{i=1}^{n},
  \quad n = \min\!\left(k,\,
    \min\!\left\{m : \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^m p_i \ge \alpha\right\}\right)
\]
The $\alpha$-$k$-bounded DTMC has one state per distinct generated string
prefix and one terminal \textsc{Rest} state per node to absorb the remaining
probability mass $1 - \sum_i p_i$.

\subsection{The \textsc{LLMChecker} Algorithm (Original)}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:original} reproduces the encoding procedure from
\cite{gross2025llmchecker} (their Algorithm~1).
It performs a recursive DFS over the token tree, issuing one LLM call per
node.
The DTMC is serialised as a PRISM program and verified offline by Storm.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Original \textsc{LLMChecker} DTMC construction (DFS)}
\label{alg:original}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\omega^{(0)}, s_\text{parent}, p_\text{parent}, k, L, M(\omega)$
\Ensure DTMC $D$ (built incrementally as PRISM code)
\State $i \gets |\omega^{(0)}|$; \quad $\textit{UID} \gets \text{fresh}()$
\If{$\omega^{(0)} = \textsc{Rest}$}
  \State $s \gets (i, \textit{UID}, \textsc{Placeholder})$
\Else
  \State $s \gets (i, \textit{UID}, M(\omega^{(0)}))$
\EndIf
\If{$s_\text{parent} \ne \textsc{null}$}
  \State $P(s_\text{parent}, s) \gets p_\text{parent}$
\EndIf
\If{$L = 0$ \textbf{or} $\omega^{(0)} = \textsc{Rest}$}  \Return  \EndIf
\State $T \gets \topka(f(\omega^{(0)}))$   \Comment{\textbf{Serial} LLM call}
\State $p_\text{sum} \gets 0$
\For{$(\sigma, p) \in T$}
  \State $\omega' \gets \omega^{(0)} \cdot \sigma$
  \State $\Call{RMC}{\omega', s, p, k, L{-}1, M}$   \Comment{DFS recursion}
  \State $p_\text{sum} \gets p_\text{sum} + p$
\EndFor
\State $\Call{RMC}{\textsc{Rest}, s, 1{-}p_\text{sum}, k, L{-}1, M}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Serial bottleneck.}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:original} makes $\sum_{d=0}^{L} k^d = \Theta(k^L)$
sequential LLM calls.
For $k=15$, $L=5$ this is $\sim\!750{,}000$ calls.
The encoding times in Table~II of~\cite{gross2025llmchecker} range from $2$
to $23{,}023$ seconds for a single verification query.

\section{BFS Construction with Batched GPU Inference}
\label{sec:bfs}

\subsection{Key Observation: Level Independence}

\begin{proposition}[Level independence]
\label{prop:level-independence}
All nodes at depth $d$ in the $\alpha$-$k$-bounded DTMC are mutually
independent: (i)~they do not share state, (ii)~they all require identical
operations (one evaluation of $\topka(f(\cdot))$), and (iii)~their
string prefixes are entirely determined before depth $d$ is processed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each node's string $\omega$ uniquely determines its position in the tree.
No transition leads from depth $d$ back to depth $d$ or earlier (the DTMC is
a directed tree), so nodes at depth $d$ are causally independent of each
other.  The LLM call $f(\omega)$ depends only on $\omega$, not on any
sibling node.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:level-independence} motivates replacing DFS with BFS:
collect \emph{all} nodes at depth $d$, submit them as a single batched
inference call, and receive all their top-$k$ distributions simultaneously.

\subsection{The BFS Algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:bfs} gives the BFS construction.
The outer loop iterates $L$ times (once per depth level), and the single LLM
call on line~\ref{line:batch} processes the entire level in parallel.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{BFS DTMC construction with batched GPU inference}
\label{alg:bfs}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\omega^{(0)}, L, \alpha, k, M(\cdot)$, backend
\Ensure levels $= [\ell_0, \ell_1, \ldots, \ell_L]$
\State $r \gets \text{DTMCNode}(\omega^{(0)}, \text{depth}=0, p=1)$
\State $\ell_0 \gets [r]$; \quad $\text{active} \gets [r]$
\For{$d = 0, \ldots, L{-}1$}
  \State $\omega\text{-batch} \gets [\text{node.string for node in active}]$
  \State $\text{results} \gets \text{backend.get\_top\_k\_batch}(\omega\text{-batch},\, \alpha,\, k)$
    \label{line:batch}
    \Comment{\textbf{Single batched GPU call for entire level $d$}}
  \State $\ell_{d+1} \gets [\ ]$; \quad $\text{next\_active} \gets [\ ]$
  \For{$(\text{node},\, (\text{tokens},\, \text{probs})) \in \text{zip}(\text{active},\, \text{results})$}
    \State $p_\text{sum} \gets 0$
    \For{$(\sigma,\, p) \in \text{zip}(\text{tokens},\, \text{probs})$}
      \State child $\gets \text{DTMCNode}(\text{node.string}+\sigma,\, d+1,\, p,\, M(\cdot))$
      \State $\ell_{d+1}.\text{append}(\text{child})$; \quad
             $\text{next\_active}.\text{append}(\text{child})$
      \State $p_\text{sum} \gets p_\text{sum} + p$
    \EndFor
    \If{$1 - p_\text{sum} > 0$}  \Comment{REST absorbing terminal}
      \State $\ell_{d+1}.\text{append}(\text{DTMCNode}(\textsc{Rest},\, d+1,\, 1{-}p_\text{sum}))$
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \State $\text{active} \gets \text{next\_active}$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Complexity Comparison}

\begin{proposition}[Serial LLM calls]
Algorithm~\ref{alg:original} requires $\Theta(k^L)$ serial LLM calls.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:bfs} requires exactly $L$ batched LLM calls.
\end{proposition}

The work performed per call is the same in both cases — evaluating $\topka(f(\omega))$ for one string.
The difference is that Algorithm~\ref{alg:bfs} performs this work for all $k^d$ strings at depth $d$ in parallel on the GPU, while Algorithm~\ref{alg:original} serialises them.

\subsection{vLLM Prefix Caching Synergy}

BFS construction exposes a further optimisation when paired with
vLLM~\cite{kwon2023efficient}'s automatic KV-cache prefix sharing
(\texttt{enable\_prefix\_caching=True}).
All $k^d$ nodes at depth $d$ share the same initial string $\omega^{(0)}$
as a common prefix.
Within a BFS level, siblings that diverged recently share a longer common
prefix.
vLLM's \emph{PagedAttention} stores KV blocks in a content-addressed cache:
any prefix whose KV blocks have already been computed is served from the
cache at zero additional cost.
This means the transformer's attention over the shared portion of the tree
is computed exactly once per prefix string, just as the DFS recursion
implicitly reused its stack state — but now across a full GPU batch.

Figure~\ref{fig:prefix-cache} illustrates the effect observed in our
experiments: at depth 2 with 25 concurrent requests sharing the prefix
\texttt{"The player won because"}, vLLM reports an input throughput of
$15{,}985$ tokens/s; at depth 3 (121 requests) this rises to
$48{,}321$ tokens/s, consistent with near-complete prefix cache hits.

\section{GPU Sparse Backward Induction}
\label{sec:backward}

\subsection{The Tree DTMC is Acyclic}

\begin{proposition}[DTMC is a tree]
\label{prop:tree}
The $\alpha$-$k$-bounded LLM text generation DTMC produced by
Algorithm~\ref{alg:bfs} is a directed tree: every state is a unique string
$\omega$, and no transition leads from a deeper to a shallower node.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By construction, each child node's string is strictly longer than its parent's.
Since string length is a strict invariant along any path, there can be no
cycles.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No iterative solver needed]
On a tree DTMC, PCTL reachability reduces to a finite backward induction.
No iterative linear system solver (such as Storm's value iteration or
Gauss-Seidel) is required.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Backward Induction Recurrences}

Let $V_d[i]$ denote the PCTL probability at the $i$-th node of depth $d$.
The recurrences for the two operators are:

\paragraph{Eventually $\Prob(\mathbf{F}\,\phi)$.}
\begin{equation}
  V_d[i] =
  \begin{cases}
    1 & \text{if } \phi(\text{node}_i) \\
    0 & \text{if node}_i \text{ is terminal} \\
    \displaystyle\sum_j T_d[i,j]\cdot V_{d+1}[j] & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:eventually-recurrence}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Always $\Prob(\mathbf{G}\,\phi)$.}
\begin{equation}
  V_d[i] =
  \begin{cases}
    0 & \text{if } \neg\phi(\text{node}_i) \text{ or node}_i \text{ is terminal} \\
    \displaystyle\sum_j T_d[i,j]\cdot V_{d+1}[j] & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:always-recurrence}
\end{equation}

where $T_d \in \Real^{|\ell_d| \times |\ell_{d+1}|}$ is the sparse transition
matrix at depth $d$ with $T_d[i,j] = P(\text{node}_i \to \text{node}_j)$.

\subsection{GPU Implementation}

Each backward step is:
\[
  V_d \;\gets\; T_d \cdot V_{d+1}
  \quad \text{followed by}\quad
  V_d[\text{satisfying}] \gets 1, \quad V_d[\text{terminal}] \gets 0
\]
The matrix-vector product $T_d \cdot V_{d+1}$ is a single \texttt{torch.sparse.mm}
call on GPU.
$T_d$ is stored in COO sparse format; for $k=5$, $L=7$, the deepest level has
at most $5^7 = 78{,}125$ nodes and $5^7 \cdot 5 \approx 390{,}000$ non-zeros,
well within the memory budget of a consumer GPU.

The total number of GPU kernel launches is $L$ (one per depth level), compared
to Storm's iterative solver which requires $O(|S|)$ matrix-vector products to
converge.
In practice this reduces verification time from \emph{hours} (Storm, CPU) to
\emph{milliseconds} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:bwd}, GPU).

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{GPU sparse backward induction}
\label{alg:bwd}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require levels $= [\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_L]$, query (operator + predicate $\phi$)
\Ensure $V_0[0] = \Prob(\mathbf{F}/\mathbf{G}\;\phi)$ at the root
\State Initialise $V_L[i] \gets \phi(\text{node}_i)$ for all $i$ in $\ell_L$
\For{$d = L{-}1 \;\text{downto}\; 0$}
  \State Build sparse $T_d$ (COO format) from $\ell_d$ children lists
  \State $V_d \gets T_d \cdot V_{d+1}$  \Comment{GPU \texttt{torch.sparse.mm}}
  \State $V_d[i] \gets 1$ for all $i$ with $\phi(\ell_d[i])$ and operator $= \mathbf{F}$
  \State $V_d[i] \gets 0$ for all $i$ with $\neg\phi(\ell_d[i])$ and operator $= \mathbf{G}$
  \State $V_d[i] \gets 0$ for all terminal $\ell_d[i]$
\EndFor
\Return $V_0[0]$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Direct Statistical Model Checking for Long Lookaheads}
\label{sec:dsmc}

\subsection{Motivation}

Even with BFS and GPU backward induction, the state space $\Theta(k^L)$ is the
fundamental barrier.
For $L = 30$, $k = 5$: $5^{30} \approx 9.3 \times 10^{20}$ states.
For $L = 50$, $k = 5$: $5^{50} \approx 8.9 \times 10^{34}$ states.
No algorithm can enumerate these.

However, PCTL reachability probabilities can be \emph{estimated} without
enumerating the state space, using \emph{Statistical Model Checking}
(SMC)~\cite{younes2002probabilistic,clarke2011model}.
Classical SMC traces \emph{sampled paths} through the DTMC and uses
hypothesis testing or confidence intervals to estimate probabilities.

The \textsc{LLMChecker} paper does not implement or discuss SMC.
We introduce \emph{Direct SMC}, which goes further: rather than sampling paths
through an explicit DTMC (which still requires building the tree), we sample
directly from the LLM's generation process.

\subsection{Direct SMC Algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Direct SMC for long-lookahead PCTL}
\label{alg:dsmc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\omega^{(0)}, L, \phi, M(\cdot), \text{backend}, M_\text{samples}, \delta$
\Ensure Estimate $\hat{p}$ and confidence interval $[\hat{p}{-}\eps,\, \hat{p}{+}\eps]$
\State $\text{count} \gets 0$
\For{each chunk of $B$ trajectories}
  \State $\{(\sigma^{(m)}_1, \ldots, \sigma^{(m)}_L)\}_{m=1}^{B}
      \gets \text{backend.generate}(\omega^{(0)},\; L,\; n{=}B)$
      \Comment{\textbf{One GPU call for $B$ complete trajectories}}
  \For{$m = 1, \ldots, B$}
    \State $\text{sat} \gets \Call{Evaluate}{\omega^{(0)},
        (\sigma^{(m)}_1,\ldots,\sigma^{(m)}_L), \phi, M(\cdot)}$
    \If{sat}\ $\text{count} \gets \text{count} + 1$\ \EndIf
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\State $\hat{p} \gets \text{count} / M_\text{samples}$
\State $\eps \gets \sqrt{\ln(2/\delta)\;/\;(2 M_\text{samples})}$
  \Comment{Chernoff–Hoeffding bound}
\Return $\hat{p},\; \max(0, \hat{p}{-}\eps),\; \min(1, \hat{p}{+}\eps)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The function \textsc{Evaluate} walks a single trajectory
$(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_L)$ and evaluates:
\begin{align*}
  \text{sat} &= \bigvee_{t=1}^{L}\phi\bigl(M(\omega^{(0)}\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_t),\, t\bigr)
  & &\text{for } \Prob(\mathbf{F}\,\phi) \\
  \text{sat} &= \bigwedge_{t=1}^{L}\phi\bigl(M(\omega^{(0)}\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_t),\, t\bigr)
  & &\text{for } \Prob(\mathbf{G}\,\phi)
\end{align*}

\subsection{Statistical Guarantee}

\begin{theorem}[Chernoff–Hoeffding confidence bound]
\label{thm:chernoff}
Let $p = \Prob(\mathbf{F}/\mathbf{G}\;\phi)$ be the true PCTL probability.
Let $\hat{p} = \text{count}/M$ be the Direct SMC estimate from $M$ i.i.d.\
trajectory samples.
For any $\delta \in (0,1)$:
\[
  \Prob\!\left(|\hat{p} - p| \le \sqrt{\frac{\ln(2/\delta)}{2M}}\right) \ge 1 - \delta
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each trajectory evaluation is an independent Bernoulli trial with success
probability $p$.
Hoeffding's inequality~\cite{hoeffding1963} applied to the bounded $[0,1]$
random variable gives the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sample complexity]
To achieve additive error $\eps$ at confidence $1-\delta$, it suffices to draw
$M \ge \ln(2/\delta) / (2\eps^2)$ samples.
For $\eps = 0.025$ (2.5\%) and $\delta = 0.05$ (95\% confidence):
$M \ge 3{,}689$ samples.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{vLLM Integration: Native Multi-Sequence Generation}

The key GPU optimisation for Direct SMC is vLLM's
$\texttt{SamplingParams(n=}B\texttt{)}$ option, which generates $B$ independent
completions of a single prompt in one forward-pass session.
All $B$ trajectories share the KV cache for the prompt $\omega^{(0)}$ (computed
once) and diverge only in the sampled tokens.
This is more efficient than $B$ separate \texttt{generate} calls, as the prompt
prefill is amortised.

\subsection{Complexity}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Probabilistic model checking.}
Model checking for DTMCs and MDPs is a mature field~\cite{baier2008principles}.
Tools including PRISM~\cite{kwiatkowska2011prism4} and Storm~\cite{hensel2022storm}
implement exact PCTL verification via value iteration and linear programming.
GPU acceleration of model checking has been studied for specific structures
(e.g.\ BDD-based symbolic methods~\cite{hermanns2008symbolic}) but not, to our
knowledge, for the sparse backward induction we describe.

\paragraph{Statistical model checking.}
SMC replaces exact verification with simulation-based estimation, providing
statistical guarantees~\cite{younes2002probabilistic,legay2010statistical}.
The UPPAAL SMC tool~\cite{bulychev2012uppaal} and the SMC extension of PRISM
both implement SMC for continuous-time models.
We extend this line of work to LLM text generation, where the ``simulator''
is the LLM itself.

\paragraph{LLM verification.}
There is growing interest in formal verification of neural network
behaviours~\cite{huang2017safety}.
Semantic robustness for NLP~\cite{malfa2022king} and robustness
verification for transformers~\cite{shi2020robustness,liao2023robust} focus on
input perturbations rather than generation dynamics.
\textsc{LLMChecker}~\cite{gross2025llmchecker} is, to our knowledge, the first
to apply PCTL model checking to the sequential generation process.
Our work addresses its scalability limitations.

\paragraph{LLM inference optimisation.}
vLLM~\cite{kwon2023efficient} introduces PagedAttention for memory-efficient
KV cache management, enabling high-throughput batch inference.
Its automatic prefix caching~\cite{zheng2023efficiently} is the enabling
technology for our BFS level batching.
Speculative decoding~\cite{leviathan2023fast} and tree-structured
generation~\cite{cai2024medusa} also exploit the tree structure of beam search,
but for latency reduction rather than probabilistic verification.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{When to use exact vs.\ Direct SMC.}
For $L \le 7$ and $k_\text{eff} \le 5$ (achievable with tight $\alpha$), exact
backward induction provides machine-precision probabilities in milliseconds and
is the recommended approach.
For $L > 8$ or high-entropy models (large $k_\text{eff}$), Direct SMC with
$M \ge 2{,}000$ gives ±3\% accuracy at 95\% confidence in seconds and should be
preferred.

\paragraph{Quantification function quality.}
The PCTL results are only as meaningful as the quantification function $M(\cdot)$.
Our gender bias counter uses a fixed word list; a misogynistic text that avoids
those specific words would not be flagged.
More robust quantifiers (e.g.\ embedding-based classifiers) can be plugged in
without changing the verification infrastructure, at the cost of slower
per-node evaluation.

\paragraph{Temperature and the modelled distribution.}
We set temperature $= 1.0$ throughout, using the unmodified LLM distribution.
The framework applies to any temperature, but the certified probabilities are
temperature-specific: a property that holds with high probability at $T=1$
may not hold at $T=2$.

\paragraph{Context window limit for Direct SMC.}
Direct SMC is bounded by the model's context window.
For GPT-2 ($1{,}024$ tokens), the maximum $L$ is approximately $1{,}014$ given a
ten-token prompt.
Modern models (Llama-3: $131{,}072$ tokens; Gemini 1.5: $10^6$ tokens) make
this constraint practically irrelevant.

\paragraph{REST states and probability mass.}
Exact backward induction treats the REST terminal as an absorbing state with
probability equal to the un-modelled mass $1 - \sum_i p_i$.
Direct SMC samples from the \emph{true} LLM distribution, so REST states are
never artificially introduced — Direct SMC certificates are statements about
the full untruncated distribution, while exact results are certified only for
the $\alpha$-$k$-bounded approximation.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We identified and exploited two structural properties of the
$\alpha$-$k$-bounded LLM text generation DTMC that were left unaddressed by
\textsc{LLMChecker}~\cite{gross2025llmchecker}: the independence of nodes at
the same depth, and the acyclic tree topology.
The former enables BFS batching that reduces serial LLM calls from $\Theta(k^L)$
to $L$; the latter enables GPU sparse backward induction that replaces Storm's
iterative solver with $L$ sparse matrix–vector products taking milliseconds.

For lookahead depths where the $\Theta(k^L)$ state space is itself the barrier,
we introduced Direct SMC: a novel algorithm that samples $M$ complete
trajectories directly from the LLM using vLLM's native multi-sequence GPU
batching, and evaluates PCTL properties on each sampled trajectory with
Chernoff–Hoeffding confidence bounds.
Direct SMC scales to any $L$ within the model's context window, operates on the
true untruncated LLM distribution, and is embarrassingly parallel.

On a consumer RTX~4070 we check PCTL properties at $L=50$ tokens in $\le 16$
seconds with $\pm 2.5\%$ confidence — a regime where exact verification would
require enumerating $\sim 10^{34}$ states.
